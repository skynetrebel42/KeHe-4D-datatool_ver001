±Bias %
Bias = (Forecast − Actual) ÷ Forecast × 100, per periodPositive = KeHE over-forecast (actual came in lower). Negative = under-forecast (actual beat the forecast).
●Trend rule & status (stable / warning / critical)
Trigger: avg(bias, last 2 periods) > 15%, same direction both periods → correction = current period's forecast × (1 ∓ 0.6 × |avg bias|). Critical above 30% avg bias, else warning.One bad period alone never triggers it — the rule specifically wants two periods running the same way, to rule out noise.
→Outlook — next 3 periods
Only renders when the trend rule above is triggered; flat-repeats that same corrected value for periods +1, +2, +3.Assumes KeHE's own forecast stays flat and today's bias persists — a continuation estimate, not a new demand forecast.
↻Seasonality
Pearson correlation of actual demand in the same period across "full years" (11+ of 13 periods present). Recurring = avg corr ≥0.5 across 2+ full years.Not seasonal = full years exist but don't correlate. Insufficient history = fewer than 2 full years exist yet.
⚡Anomalies — Spike / Plunge
Actual deviates 25%+ from the trailing 3-period average of actual demand.Flags a sudden jump or drop relative to recent activity, in either direction.
▲▼Anomalies — Peak / Valley
The single highest / lowest actual value ever recorded for this brand (global max/min).A separate rule from Spike/Plunge, since a gradual build-up to a record high may never cross the 25% trailing-average threshold.
◐Evidence tiers — Documented / Inferred / Unknown
Documented = KeHE's own note pins an exact period. Inferred = note describes a broader date range the timing lines up with, not pinned exactly. Unknown = no note covers it.Unknown periods are handed to the demand planner as a research prompt — the tool never guesses a cause.

Problem we set out to solve
KeHE's own forecast doesn't always match what customers actually buy, and today that gap gets caught late and informally, with no consistent way to tell a normal seasonal swing apart from a real forecasting miss. This tool gives KeHE's demand planners a fast, evidence-based read — per brand — on whether current demand is following a known seasonal pattern, drifting from the forecast persistently enough to act on, or standing out from recent history in a way worth a second look. It does that using only KeHE's own historical numbers and KeHE's own written notes, without inventing a cause the data can't support.
Quick answers
1. What's the purpose of this tool?
To help a KeHE demand planner quickly tell three things apart for Brand X, Y, or Z: whether demand follows a repeating seasonal pattern, whether KeHE's forecast has been persistently too high or too low over the last two periods, and whether a specific period stood out as a spike, plunge, all-time high, or all-time low. It turns that into one plain-language status and buy recommendation per brand — a starting point for the planner's judgment, not a replacement for it.
2. What are the top insights from using this tool?
One per brand, matching what the tool actually verified: Brand X shows a genuine recurring seasonal pattern (0.913 correlation between 2024 and 2025 in the same periods) and is currently tracking within normal range. Brand Y has no established seasonal pattern yet — it has never had a full 13-period year on record — and is currently running persistently over-forecast, which is the tool's clearest "reduce the buy" signal right now. Brand Z's past spike didn't repeat the following year, so it reads as a one-off event rather than something to plan around annually.
3. How should I apply this knowledge?
Use it as a first pass, not the final word. Start with the Overview status and action line, check the "Why?" math behind it, then look at whether Seasonality says the current pattern is expected to repeat before trusting a Trend-triggered correction. Treat any Unknown anomaly as a to-do, not a dead end — the Research Directive tells you exactly what to go check before acting on it. The tool tells you where to look and what the numbers say; confirming the real-world cause is still on you.
4. How was the data rule established and proofread?
The trend rule (average bias over the last 2 periods, 15%/30% thresholds, 60% correction) was built around one goal set at the start of this project: flag a persistent same-direction miss, not a single bad period. It was verified by hand — pulling actual and forecast numbers directly from KeHE's original spreadsheet independently of the tool's own generated output, recalculating the bias and trend math by hand, and confirming it matched what the tool displayed, specifically to avoid checking the tool's math against itself. Every threshold used across the dashboard, tooltips, and Legend was also cross-checked line by line for internal consistency in a separate proofreading pass.
5. Why didn't you use outside sources like Google Trends?
Two reasons. KeHE never confirmed what Brand X, Y, and Z actually are — "Brand X" is the team's educated guess from footnote clues, not a confirmed product name — so searching Google Trends for a guessed product risks presenting a coincidental correlation as real evidence. And KeHE's own "Demand Planner Insights" documentation already describes planners doing that external research themselves as part of their job. So the tool deliberately stops at what it can verify — KeHE's own numbers and KeHE's own notes — and hands off anything it can't explain as a specific, factual research prompt for the planner to run down, rather than guessing on their behalf.
Strengths
Every number is hand-checkable
The raw table, the Legend's exact formulas, and the hover "Why?" tooltips all trace back to the same source data — nothing shown is a black box.
One rule set, applied evenly
The same thresholds apply to all three brands — nothing was hand-tuned per brand to make results look cleaner than they are.
Independently verified
Checked against a fresh hand calculation from the raw spreadsheet, not just against the tool's own generated numbers.
Never invents a cause
An anomaly is only ever explained by KeHE's own note, or explicitly flagged Unknown with a concrete next step — never a guessed explanation dressed up as fact.
Limitations
Thresholds are reasoned starting points, not statistically fitted
The 25% spike/plunge cutoff and the 15%/30% trend bands are deliberate first guesses, not values fitted or validated against a larger dataset. Treat a flagged severity as "worth a look," not gospel.
Documented / Inferred / Unknown reflects what KeHE wrote, not what's true
If KeHE knew a cause but never noted it, the tool has no way to know that — it can only see what's on the page.
Peak/Valley rests on one data point
It's the single all-time high or low value on record. One data-entry error in the spreadsheet would get flagged exactly like a real record.
Seasonality needs history the newer brands don't have yet
Calling a verdict needs 2+ "full" years (11 of 13 periods). A shorter-history brand like Brand Y will always read "not enough history yet," even if a real pattern is starting to form.
The Outlook assumes nothing changes
It holds today's bias and KeHE's forecast flat. It breaks the moment KeHE issues a new forecast or the underlying driver shifts — which is exactly why it's labeled "Outlook," not "prediction."
Tricky parts — easy to misread
Bias % sign is easy to flip in your head
Positive means KeHE over-forecast (actual came in lower). Negative means under-forecast (actual came in higher). It's a direction, not a "good vs. bad" score.
A gradual climb to a record won't necessarily show up as a "Spike"
That's exactly why Peak/Valley is a separate rule from Spike/Plunge, not a subset of it — a slow build-up may never cross the 25% trailing-average threshold.
Documented / Inferred / Unknown is a citation tier, not a confidence score
"Documented" means KeHE's note pins the exact period — it doesn't mean the tool is "100% sure." There's no numeric confidence anywhere in this tool, on purpose.
An empty Outlook card isn't a bug
It only renders a projection when a trend is currently triggered. Seeing "no active bias trigger" just means nothing is currently flagged for that brand.
"Full year" doesn't mean literally all 13 periods
Seasonality counts a year as "full" at 11 of 13 periods present, so a year missing one or two records due to a late entry still counts toward a verdict.
